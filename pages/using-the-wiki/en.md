## A guide and a game record answer different questions

An item page is useful when you already know what you need: it brings together the matching recipe, recorded sources and links to related objects. A wiki guide connects those records into an explanation. Start with the guide when the process is unfamiliar, then open the linked game objects for the current details.

The original guides here were written for this wiki from observed release-index records. They are not copies of another community wiki. Each article identifies its references so you can distinguish the editorial explanation from the game data shown beside it.

## Read live components in context

A recipe or creature card is loaded from an indexed dataset rather than copied into the paragraph. Its patchline and dataset context matter. The written explanation retains its source revision; a live component can reflect a later extraction. Missing data means the index does not establish that value, rather than a zero or an impossible event.

## Suggest a correction

Sign in to save a private draft. The editor offers a preview before you submit a public suggestion. Submitting makes the proposed text and chosen public alias visible in the content repository. Editors can review suggestions; publication is a separate step from saving a draft.

Keep a source for factual changes. Explain conditions and uncertainty instead of turning a recorded reference into a guaranteed drop, spawn or crafting outcome.
